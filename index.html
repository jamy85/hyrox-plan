Load rule — what replaced “RIR 3”

Mon 20 Jul at 20 kg goblet / 2×10 kg lunge / 30 kg RDL produced 7–8/10 DOMS across quads, glutes and hams, and cost you the Wednesday brick. That is the calibration point, so the kilos are now decided by the previous week’s soreness:

  • 0–4/10, gone by Wednesday → add ~2 kg lower body.
  • 5–6/10, or still stiff Wednesday → repeat the same load.
  • 7–8/10, or it cost you a session → drop ~2 kg.
  • Missed last Wednesday → never add load this week, whatever the soreness.

Within a given load, still stop ~3 reps short of failure. The gym exists so your legs hold up at the stations — a Monday that costs you Wednesday is a net loss no matter how good the lift felt.

You can’t predict DOMS during the session — so don’t try

How hard a session feels tells you about fatigue, not muscle damage. They are separate things, which is why 20 Jul felt manageable and then flattened you two days later. Soreness peaks 24–72 h out, and your Wednesday brick sits right on the peak.

Two things you can control on Monday, because they drive damage far more than the weight does:

  • Range — don’t go deeper than prescribed on lunges; stop the RDL bar at mid-shin.
  • Tempo — don’t add slow negatives. Lower under control, not slowly.

Then move the decision to Tuesday morning, when soreness is finally readable and Wednesday is still saveable. Walk down a flight of stairs, do 5 slow bodyweight squats, score the worse of the two:

5–6/10Brick Lite — keep every 800 m run, cut to 2 rounds, drop the lunge

Decide Tuesday, not Wednesday. By Wednesday you are at peak soreness and will talk yourself out of it. And note Brick Lite keeps the runs: running is the least aggravating thing you can do on sore legs, and compromised running is the actual race skill — so a sore week should cost you volume, never the whole session.

Fuel & recovery

Daily targets

  • Protein — ~1.6–2.2 g/kg/day, split into 3–4 feeds of ~35–40 g. Your #1 recovery lever.
  • Carbs — 300–450 g on training days; top end on hard days (Wed/Sun), lower on easy/rest.
  • Fat — ~60–90 g; fill the rest of your energy here, don’t go too low.
  • Fluid — 2.5–3.5 L+/day, more in the heat; add sodium on sweaty morning runs.
  • Eat enough — you’re fuelling performance, not cutting; don’t let bodyweight drift down.
Protein
  • Repairs the muscle you’re re-loading (what clears DOMS faster) and builds the strength-endurance gap.
  • Spread beats one big hit — one dose within ~2 h after training, one before bed (yogurt/casein).
  • Sources: eggs, chicken/fish, Greek yogurt, dairy, tofu/tempeh, legumes, whey.
Carbs — periodise
  • 800s + brick run on glycogen — don’t low-carb while doing intervals.
  • More on Wed/Sun, fewer on easy/rest days.
Energy

Under-eating is a classic hidden cause of the fatigue / EF-drift pattern you had. Load goes up → food goes up. Bodyweight holding steady = about right.

Around your sessions

  • Before hard AM sessions — a small easy carb (banana, toast + honey, oats). Easy runs are fine fasted.
  • After hard sessions — carbs + protein together to refill glycogen and start repair.
  • Sweaty runs — ~400–500 ml fluid beforehand; electrolytes/sodium for the longer, hotter ones.
  • Weigh before/after a run; drink ~1.25–1.5× the weight lost afterward.
  • Pale-yellow urine = about right. Dark = behind; clear-and-frequent = over-drinking.
  • SG heat means more sweat + sodium loss than the numbers suggest — salt your food, don’t fear it.

Recovery & DOMS

Score your soreness each Tuesday/Wednesday out of 10 — that number is what sets Monday’s weights (see the load rule above). 20 Jul was a 7–8 and cost a session; the aim is to keep it at 4 or under.

  • Hit protein every day — the single best thing for clearing DOMS.
  • Don’t skimp on calories — give your body materials to repair.
  • Omega-3s (oily fish 2×/week or a supplement) + colourful whole foods help the inflammatory load.
  • Go easy on routine NSAIDs (ibuprofen) for DOMS — regular use can blunt strength adaptation; occasional is fine.
  • Sleep is the multiplier on all of it. Tart cherry juice is a minor, optional extra.

Race week & morning

  • Taper — light carb emphasis the last 2–3 days (volume is down, so don’t overeat — just favour carbs).
  • Race morning — tested breakfast 2–3 h before (carbs + a little protein, low fibre/fat) + fluid & sodium.
  • Caffeine ~45–60 min before is a legit boost if you tolerate it.
  • Nothing new — rehearse breakfast + hydration on a W5 hard session. Full pacing in the Race-day playbook below.

How to read this

Easy Moderate Hard / Race

Effort is always a number here — Easy = under 138 bpm. Moderate = 150–161 bpm. Hard = 3:56 per 800 or 15.0 km/h on the belt. Nothing in this plan says just “go hard”; if a session feels vague, check Your numbers above.

Load rule — weights are gated on how sore last Monday left you, not on the week number: under 4/10 and cleared by Wednesday → add ~2 kg; 5–6/10 → repeat; 7–8/10 → drop ~2 kg. Within any load, stop ~3 reps short of failure. Full rule in the Your numbers section.

Substitutions built in — ski → cable/DB pulling, sled → incline-treadmill or ramp/stairs push, wall ball → DB thrusters. Race-day playbook · Sat 29 Aug

Pacing & execution

  • Don't blow the first 800. It'll feel easy — that's the trap. Bank nothing; run it at goal, not below.
  • Expect the leg tax. After sled, lunges and wall balls your legs turn over slower — accept ~20–40 s/km on the next run and just keep the cadence up.
  • Thrusters & wall balls: steady rhythm, and break on purpose. A steady rhythm beats fast-then-stalling. Unbroken is worth having when it comes free, but the first thing to give out for you is the triceps lockout — so plan a short break (rack 3–5 s) before it stalls rather than grinding into one. Drive from the legs; breathe at the top.
  • Row legs-first, strong but not redlined — it's easy to overcook and wreck the next run.
  • Sled / incline push: short powerful steps, low body angle, keep moving.
  • Negative-split mindset: aim to finish the back half no slower than the front.

Logistics

  • Warm-up: 10 min easy jog, dynamic legs, 3–4 strides, a few thrusters + 100 m row to prime — finish ~10 min before start.
  • Fuel: normal breakfast 2–3 h prior; it's a short event, no mid-race fuel needed. Hydrate to pale-yellow, don't over-drink.
  • Nothing new on race day — shoes, kit, breakfast all pre-tested in week 5.

Guardrails

  • Moderate loads weeks 1–2 — reintroducing gym work; DOMS will otherwise eat your Sunday quality.
  • Never two hard days back-to-back without a rest day between (that pattern caused the early-July efficiency drift).
  • Watch HR & EF on easy + interval runs in the tracker — a creeping HR-at-pace means under-recovered; back off.
  • Taper is training too. Week 6 volume drops ~50%; you gain freshness and lose nothing. Resist adding "one more" session.
  • A light runner is advantaged everywhere except the sled — that's your relative weak point, so the incline-push sub and leg strength-endurance earn their keep.

Loads are starting points, not prescriptions — autoregulate to RIR 2–3 and progress when a weight feels easy at the target reps. Rower is available every Monday + on even weeks' Fridays (W2, W4); odd-week Fridays use the seated bike (or hard runs) — same effort. The week-5 dress rehearsal sits on Mon 17 Aug so it includes a real row. Pace targets firm up once you log your first 800s set.
